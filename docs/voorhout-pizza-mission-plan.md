# Plan — "Hot Slice": open-world-game style ad for New York Pizza Voorhout

Status: PLAN, not started. Written for a fresh session to execute end to end.
Read `CLAUDE.md` first (doctrine) and append the result to `docs/lab-notebook.md` when done.

## 1. Goal and definition of done

A 40–50 second vertical (9:16, 1080x1920) video that looks like a photorealistic
open-world crime-game mission set in the real Voorhout: a delivery rider leaves
New York Pizza on the Herenstraat, gets stopped by the closing barriers at the
station level crossing while a train passes, then delivers the pizza on time.
The viewer reaction we want is "wow, that is my village in a game".

Done means:

1. `press/nyp-voorhout-hot-slice.mp4` exists, 1080x1920, H.264 + AAC, 40–50 s,
   integrated loudness -14 LUFS (repo convention, see commit `12017d8`).
2. It has: 7 generated shots, game HUD overlay, music bed, sound effects,
   real shop logo on the end card.
3. A local viewer who knows Voorhout recognises the shop front and the crossing
   (the user is the judge).
4. Lab notebook entry appended.

## 2. Hard constraints (do not relitigate)

- **No Rockstar IP.** No "GTA", no "Jason", no game logo, no Pricedown-style
  title font, no copied HUD layout, no music from the game. It is an original
  parody of the genre. Hero is an original character (working name: Daan).
- **Hero waits at the crossing.** He never dodges barriers or races the train.
- **Veo is driven by the user in Google Flow.** Flow has no API; no agent can
  run it. The agent prepares prompts and does everything after the clips exist.
- **No text or logos generated by Veo.** All text, HUD and the logo are added
  in the edit. Veo distorts them.
- **No readable real number plates or recognisable bystanders** in start-frame
  photos or final frames.
- **Free tools first:** ffmpeg, Playwright/Chromium (preinstalled), royalty-free
  audio. No paid service without asking the user.

## 4. Realism bible (put the relevant lines in every prompt)

- **Streets:** red-brown brick paving (klinkers), red asphalt cycle lanes, white
  "shark teeth" give-way triangles, low brick terraced houses with orange tile
  roofs, blue street-name signs, many parked bicycles.
- **Vehicles:** small European hatchbacks and vans. Dutch plates: yellow
  rectangle, black characters, blue EU strip on the left. Keep plates soft or
  out of focus so no text is readable.
- **Scooter:** red electric delivery scooter with a rear insulated box and a
  small yellow moped plate. Logo on the box is added in the edit.
- **Crossing:** red and white striped half barriers, white St Andrew's cross
  sign with red edges, two alternating red warning lights, ringing bell,
  overhead electric wires above two tracks.
- **Train:** yellow Dutch passenger train with blue and white bands.
- **Landscape:** flat, big sky, straight ditches, bulb fields (the game world
  is set in spring: tulips and hyacinths in bloom, even though we shoot in November).
- **Light:** low golden-hour sun, long shadows, slightly heightened colour.

## 5. Phase A — Human steps (user, in Voorhout and in Flow)

**A1. Verify the route in Street View (10 min).** Open these and screenshot nothing; just confirm the layout and tell the agent what is different from section 3:

- Shop: `https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&viewpoint=52.22193,4.48234`
- Station crossing: `https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&viewpoint=52.2242,4.4844`

**A2. Take 5 phone photos, vertical, around sunset** (own photos, not Street View
screenshots, which carry Google's copyright and watermark):

1. Shop front straight on, logo visible, door closed.
2. Shop front from across the street, three-quarter angle.
3. Herenstraat looking toward the crossing, from about 50 m away.
4. The crossing from the stop line, barriers up, no train.
5. A residential front door or street on the far side (with the resident's OK), or a field edge.

**A3. Get from the shop:** approval for the video, the official logo file
(SVG or PNG), the offer and order link for the end card.

**A4. Generate in Flow** with Veo, 9:16, 8 s per shot, audio on. Order:
hero image, then shots 4 and 5 (hardest), then the rest. Two takes per shot.
Use the hero image as an ingredient in every shot and the listed photo as the
start frame. Save as `raw/s1_a.mp4`, `raw/s1_b.mp4`, … and upload to the session.

> Photorealistic next-gen video game character render of Daan, a Dutch pizza

> helmet under his arm, standing beside a red electric delivery scooter with a
> plain insulated box on the back, Dutch brick village street at golden hour,

## 6. Phase B — Agent steps (after clips are uploaded)

Sequential unless marked parallel. Git work stays in the main session.

**B1. Ingest and pick takes.** `ffprobe` every clip, extract 4 frames each,
view them, pick the better take per shot against the reject list in section 7.
Report rejects to the user with the reason and a corrected prompt.

**B2. Build the HUD (parallel-safe, three independent pieces).** Original design,
rendered as transparent PNG sequences or WebM with Playwright from HTML/CSS, then
overlaid with ffmpeg.

- *Minimap:* circular, bottom left, drawn from real OpenStreetMap street
  geometry around 52.2219–52.2245 N, 4.480–4.487 E, route line from the shop to
  the crossing, player arrow that advances per shot. Fetch the geometry through
  the Firecrawl scrape tool on an Overpass API URL (direct curl is blocked by the
  proxy). Put "© OpenStreetMap contributors" in the video description.
- *Mission text and timer:* title card "HOT SLICE" on shot 1, objective
  "Bezorg de pizza voordat hij koud wordt" on shot 2, countdown timer top right
  that turns red on shot 4 and resumes on shot 5, "Obstakel gepasseerd" on shot 5.
- *End card:* "MISSIE GESLAAGD", real logo file, address
  "Herenstraat 112, Voorhout", the offer and order link from A3. 3 seconds.

Use an open-licensed display font (for example Anton or Bebas Neue from Google
Fonts if reachable; otherwise a system bold condensed face).

**B3. Assemble.** Trim each shot to 5–6.5 s on the action, hard cuts, order 1–7,
end card last. Target 42–48 s. Scale and pad to 1080x1920 at 30 fps.

**B4. Sound.**

- Keep Veo's native audio as the effects layer.
- Music bed: one royalty-free synthwave or West Coast style instrumental,
  around 100–110 BPM, from a free library with a licence that allows
  commercial use (record title, author, licence URL in the notebook). If no
  library is reachable, ask the user to drop a track in `raw/music.mp3`.
- Duck the music under the crossing bell and train on shots 4–5 (sidechain or
  volume automation), bring it back hard on the cut to shot 6.
- Add UI sounds synthesised with ffmpeg: mission-start sting, timer tick on
  shot 4, success chime on the end card.
- Normalise the mix to -14 LUFS, true peak -1 dB.

**B5. Verify (a different agent than the one that built it).** Give it the
output file and section 1 and 7, tell it to refute: check duration, resolution,
loudness with `ffmpeg -af ebur128`, extract one frame per second and inspect
for garbled text, wrong logo, readable plates, HUD overlapping faces, and any
frame where the hero crosses closed barriers.

**B6. Ship.** Save to `press/`, commit, push to the working branch, send the
file to the user. Publishing to social media is the user's call, not the agent's.

## 7. Reject list for takes

- Hero's face or outfit changes between shots.
- Any invented text, logo or sign that reads as gibberish.
- Barriers pass through objects, or the hero moves while they are down.
- Train on the road side of the barriers, or driving on the wrong kind of track.
- Cars on the left side of the road, non-European cars, white or US plates.
- Hills, palm trees or anything that is not flat Dutch landscape.

## 8. Law 1 record (fill the result in afterwards)

- **Constraint:** human generation time in Flow, since nothing upstream of the
  clips can be automated. Everything in phase B is subordinate to it: prompts
  must be right first time and rejects must come back with a fixed prompt.
- **Hypothesis:** if shots start from real photos of the Herenstraat and all
  text is added in the edit, then locals recognise the place and nothing reads
  as fake, because Veo preserves a start frame's geometry far better than it
  invents a specific village from words.
- **Prediction (written before generating):** shots 1, 2, 4 and 5 are
  recognisable to the user without being told where they are; shot 5 needs the
  most retakes (3 or more) because of train and barrier physics.
- **Cheapest falsifying test:** hero image plus shots 4 and 5 only. If the
  crossing is not recognisable from photo 4, stop and change approach before
  generating the other five shots.
- **Pre-mortem (public ad, hard to recall once posted):** most likely failures
  are the franchise objecting to logo use (covered by A3), a takedown for
  looking too much like the real game (covered by section 2), and the hero
  drifting between shots (covered by the hero ingredient and B1).
