## 1. Goal and definition of done

A 40–50 second vertical video that looks like a photorealistic open-world
crime-game mission set in the real Voorhout: a delivery rider leaves New York
Pizza on the Herenstraat, is stopped by the closing barriers at the station
level crossing while a train passes, then delivers the pizza on time. The
reaction we want from locals: "wow, that is my village in a game".

Done means:

1. `ads/nyp-voorhout/out/nyp-voorhout-hot-slice.mp4`: 1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264
   (yuv420p, faststart) + AAC 48 kHz, 40–50 s, integrated loudness -14 LUFS
   (±0.5), true peak ≤ -1 dBTP.
2. The first 2 seconds show the crossing with the train, never a man reading a phone.
3. At least three real place names appear on screen (for example "Herenstraat",
   "Station Voorhout", "Voorhout").
4. Game HUD, music bed, sound effects, real shop logo on the mission card and end card.
5. The owner recognises the shop front and the crossing without being told.
6. A reusable driver exists in `tools/flow_driver/` so the next ad is a manifest change.
7. Lab notebook entry appended.

## 2. Hard constraints (do not relitigate)

**Content**

- **No Rockstar IP.** No "GTA", no "Jason", no game logo, no Pricedown-style
  title font, no copied HUD layout, no wanted-star meter, no music from the
  game. Original parody of the genre with an original hero (working name Daan).
- **Hero waits at the crossing.** He never passes a lowering or closed barrier
  and never races the train.
- **No text or logos drawn by the model.** All text, HUD and logos are added in
  the edit. The real shop sign may appear only where it comes from the owner's
  own footage and the camera is static.
- **No readable number plates, no recognisable bystanders, no house numbers or name plates.**
- **Shop approval is a gate, not a note** (section 6).

**Money**

- Credits are real money. Every paid generation (still, video, re-render) goes
  through the single spend path in section 8c and a gate in section 9.
- Costs are read live and measured from the balance. Never assume them.

**Chrome safety** (the driver attaches to the owner's real browser session)

- Never call `browser.close()`, `context.close()`, or `page.close()` on a page the driver did not open.
- Never run `pkill`, `kill` or `killall`. Never call `nexy_v2`'s Python launch
  helpers: `core/browser_manager.py:133` and `utils/browser.py:181` kill Chrome by port.
- Work only in a page the driver opened with `context.new_page()`; never touch
  tabs whose URL is not under `labs.google/fx`. On exit, disconnect and leave Chrome running.
- One Google account at human pace: one generation in flight, at least 45 s
  between submissions. No stealth, fingerprint or multi-profile modules.

**Repos**

- Do not edit or write into `~/Documents/nexy_v2`. Read it; copy techniques into this repo.
- Free tools first: ffmpeg, Playwright, royalty-free or synthesised audio.

## 4. Realism bible (already folded into `shots.json`; keep when rewriting prompts)

- **Streets:** red-brown brick paving, red asphalt cycle lanes, white "shark
  teeth" give-way triangles, low brick terraced houses with orange tile roofs,
  blue street-name signs, parked bicycles. Traffic keeps right.
- **Vehicles:** small European hatchbacks and vans; Dutch plates are yellow.
  Do not mention plates in video prompts (it invites gibberish text); hide them in the keyframe.
- **Scooter:** red electric delivery scooter with a plain, unbranded insulated
  box. No logo on the box: tracking a logo onto a moving object is not worth it.
- **Rider:** helmet on whenever he rides.
- **Crossing:** red and white striped half barriers, St Andrew's cross sign,
  two alternating red lights, ringing bell, overhead wires above two tracks.
- **Train:** yellow Dutch passenger train with blue and white bands.
- **Landscape:** flat, big sky, straight ditches. The game world is set in
  spring (tulips in bloom) even though we shoot in November.
- **Light:** low golden-hour sun, long shadows, slightly heightened colour.
- **Game feel** comes from the third-person camera in shots 3–7 and from the
  HUD, not from making the picture look rendered.

### 8b. Decide the character-consistency route (prediction written first)

Prediction: Flow will not accept a start frame and a character reference in the
same video generation. If so use **route K**; if it does, route D.

- **Route K (keyframes, preferred).** For each shot whose `start_frame.type` is
  `keyframe`, make a still with Flow's image generation: the location frame +
  `keyframes/hero.png` + `keyframe_instruction` + `keyframe_prompt`. Save as
  `keyframes/sN.png`. Then run frames-to-video from that still, re-uploaded from disk.
- **Route D (direct).** Start frame = location frame, reference = hero sheet, one generation.
- **Route T (fallback).** If image generation with references is unavailable:
  frames-to-video from the raw location frame, with the hero described by a
  fixed sentence at the start of every prompt. Expect drift; tell the owner.

In all routes, shots 5 and 6 start from the **last frame of the chosen take of
the previous shot** (`start_frame.type: last_frame_of`), so the crossing, the
barriers and the rider carry over exactly. That makes 4 → 5 → 6 a strict
sequence: generate, choose, extract the frame, then generate the next.

In `keyframe_instruction`, "stays exactly as in the location frame" covers
buildings and road layout; the `keyframe_prompt` may change what it names.

### 8c. Build `tools/flow_driver/flow_driver.py`

One file, Playwright async, reads `shots.json`, `selectors.json` and `runs/budget.json`.

| Command | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `probe` | Attach, open own page, read balance, screenshot. Spends nothing |
| `still --hero a\|b` / `still --shot N` | Generate the hero sheet or a keyframe |
| `video --shot N --take K` | One generation, wait, download to `raw/sN_tK.mp4` |
| `run --shots 4 --takes 1,2` | Loop with pacing and resume |
| `status` | Table of shots × takes with state and ledger totals |
| `video --shot N --print-prompt` | Print the composed prompt and the start-frame path; touches no browser |

Every `still`, `video` and `run` needs either `--dry-run` (do everything up to,
not including, Create, and screenshot the filled prompt bar) or `--yes-spend`.

Required behaviour (each answers a verified failure in `nexy_v2` or a critic finding):

1. **One spend path for everything paid.** A single function
   `spend_click(kind, shot, take)` is the only code that clicks Create, for
   stills, videos and re-renders alike. It refuses unless: `--yes-spend` is
   given; the live cost label is readable; the balance is readable; the
   settings read back equal the manifest (rule 3); and
   `ledger total + projected cost ≤ ceiling` from `runs/budget.json`.
2. **Measured cost.** The real cost of a click is `balance_before − balance_after`.
   After every click compare it with the label; on any mismatch stop the run and ask the owner.
3. **Verified settings.** Expected values are exactly `format.aspect`,
   `format.outputs_per_prompt` (1), the model label for the kind, and the mode
   for the route. Set them, read them back, abort before Create on any difference
   or any missing manifest field.
4. **Write before click.** Record `{shot, take, kind, state: "submitting", balance_before, time}`
   in `runs/state.json` and flush it to disk before clicking. On restart, a take
   in `submitting` or `generating` is never resubmitted: reconcile from the
   balance and the project's tiles, then ask the owner.
5. **Completion tied to this submission.** Snapshot the result tiles before
   Create; done means a new tile with a playable video or image. Errors are read
   from that tile only. Timeout 600 s.
6. **No automatic second click, ever.** Timeout, error tile, filtered result or
   failed download sets `state: "failed"` and stops `run`. A person decides the
   next click. At most 2 attempts per shot per kind without a fresh yes.
7. **Download** with the `download_method` proven in 8a. Then `ffprobe`: 7–9 s,
   height greater than width, has an audio stream, larger than 500 KB. A failed
   check marks `download_failed`; it does not regenerate.
8. **One project for the whole ad**, reused across runs.
9. **Pacing.** One generation in flight; at least 45 s between submissions.
10. **Prompts as written**, composed per `prompt_composition` in `shots.json`. No sanitiser.
11. **Chrome safety** rules from section 2.

**Acceptance before any paid click** (still or video):

- `probe` passes.
- `still --shot 4 --dry-run` screenshot shows: image mode, the location frame
  and a stand-in hero image attached, the composed prompt, 9:16, the cost label.
- `video --shot 4 --take 1 --dry-run` screenshot shows: the right mode, a
  stand-in start frame attached (`refs/loc4_*.jpg`, since the keyframe does not
  exist yet), 9:16, one output, the model label, the prompt, the cost label.
- A second agent reviews the driver against rules 1–11 and tries to find any
  path that clicks Create twice, spends without a ledger line, or closes a page it did not open.

## 10. Reject list for stills and takes

- Hero's face, outfit or scooter changes between shots; rides without the helmet.
- Any invented text, logo or sign; the real shop sign changes shape or wording
  between the first and last frame of shots 1–2.
- Hero moves past a lowering or closed barrier; barriers pass through objects.
- Train on the road side of the barriers or off the rails.
- Traffic on the left, non-European cars, readable plates, a house number or name plate.
- A recognisable bystander face.
- Hills, palm trees or anything that is not flat Dutch landscape.

- Music or voices in the clip audio, or no audio stream.
